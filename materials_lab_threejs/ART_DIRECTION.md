# MatterLab art direction

MatterLab is a near-future but credible materials-characterization bay: clean enough to read, dense enough to feel operational, and never styled as a spaceship. The visual hierarchy is architecture and circulation first, instrument silhouettes second, utility and safety boundaries third, then samples, local screens, and status lamps.

## Visual rules

- World units are meters. Human reference height is 1.75 m and work surfaces remain near 0.90 m.
- The shell uses warm neutral walls and charcoal conductive/epoxy flooring. Equipment bodies are off-white, anodized silver, graphite, and muted teal.
- Signal color has a job: cyan is active data or navigation, green is ready/accepted, amber is attention, and red is emergency hardware. Decorative emission is avoided.
- Metals use bounded roughness and restrained clearcoat. Glass is used only for real shields, tubes, and enclosures.
- Lighting is a high-bay laboratory mix: broad neutral ceiling fixtures, a directional key, low-intensity environment fill, and small practical accents. ACESFilmic tone mapping is exposed at 0.76 to protect white housings.
- SSAO is mild and used to anchor benches and instruments. Bloom is thresholded to local screens and active stack lights. FXAA cleans long rail, cable, and cage edges.
- Review renders suppress floating UI labels. Play mode keeps labels and inspection pins because they are functional overlays, not physical props.

## Camera language

Wide shots are measured cutaways that explain circulation and equipment relationships. Human-scale shots remain at 1.65–1.75 m. Instrument judges use isolated station families so the camera evaluates the asset rather than accidental foreground occlusion. The cinematic tour follows the evidence path: facility overview → human aisle → XRD → SEM/EDS → thermal analysis → utilities → final hero.

## Acceptance rubric

Each fixed camera is scored on a 10-point scale: composition 2, scale/clearance 2, material response 2, lighting/readability 2, and operational specificity 2. A final pass requires no critical defects, no clipped primary asset, no unreadable white-on-white instrument body, consistent state colors, and a mean score of at least 8.5.
